What is simulated. A 50 lb bike built from its parts (frame, EP8 motor, 630 Wh battery, fork, cockpit, shock and links, post) so the sprung centre of mass and pitch inertia follow from them; two unsprung wheels on tyre springs (29″ front / 27.5″ rear, wheel-radius terrain enveloping) with rotational inertia and their own spin state; a 200 lb rider whose centre of mass is built from body segments (torso 50%, legs 32%, arms 10%, head 8%) at wherever the current pose puts them, on a leg spring–damper, and speed from rider power + Shimano EP8 assist against grade, rolling and aero drag, with the ground pushing along its tilted contact normal (so bump faces and lips cost speed and descents pay it back). Braking and drive go through the wheels: brake torque (65/35 front/rear) and pedal/motor torque spin the wheels against a longitudinal slip curve that peaks near 15% slip and slides at ~72% of peak, so an over-braked wheel locks (the PRO rider modulates, the AVERAGE rider does not); the tyre forces at the ground pitch the mass above them, the front tyre force also pushes the wheel up the 64° fork axis (brake dive), spinning wheels add to the mass to accelerate and resist being rolled (gyroscopic damping), and in the air brake and pedal torques react on the frame, which the PRO rider uses to level the bike. The rear wheel rides a solved four-bar VPP: lower-link-driven 230×65 shock, 170 mm at the axle, leverage ratio 3.05→2.47 (19% progressive), ~2 mm rearward then forward axle path. The fork is an EVOL-style air spring with volume tokens and a 4-way damper (LSC/HSC/LSR/HSR, knee at 0.5 m/s wheel speed); the shock is a coil with a bottom-out bumper and the same 4-way damper at the shaft.
Sources in the repo's uploads. Pinkbike's 2021 Bullit review (Kazimer): 29/27.5, 170 mm, 64° HTA, 449 mm stays, 22.3 kg size L, 475 reach on the L, Fox 38 E-Tune 170 and a RockShox Super Deluxe; his setup was 175 psi in the shock for 30% sag and 100 psi + 3 tokens in the fork at 72.6 kg. The Loam Wolf shootout bike ran the Super Deluxe Coil Ultimate (51.7 lb). Pinkbike's Megatower "Behind the Numbers" scan gives the lower-link VPP family its shape: a near-linear falling leverage curve with an end-stroke shoulder, ~2 mm rearward then forward axle path, anti-squat ~110% falling off late in the travel, anti-rise 85%→−25%. The linkage here is fitted to those outputs with the pivots and shock placed where the photos show them.
Rider posture. PRO targets come from side-on frames in crash_training/asa_vermette_vs_avg_rider_Y50K3ZZEdVY.mp4 (Asa at 185.5 s and 208.7 s: torso near trail-parallel, front knee ~135–140°, elbows ~120–150°) and the Goldstone FPV run; AVERAGE mirrors Charlie at 160.5 s and 194.5 s (upright torso, knee ~165°, stiff legs). Hip height is solved from a target knee angle and torso angle from a target elbow angle, knees always bend forward and elbows down, and caps (knee ≤165°, elbow ≤160° for PRO) stop any lock-out; the HUD bands are the downhill envelope from kinematic-model/src/disciplines.py.
World. The trail is generated from the course: every segment is a constant-curvature arc, a Hermite curve closes the loop into a ~9% return climb, a hill heightfield is bench-cut to the trail's elevation profile, and a 0.25 m trail ribbon carries the rocks, logs, washboard and the banked berm. The bike yaws with the tangent, pitches with the profile, and rolls to the lateral acceleration with a second-order response; a friction circle (μ 0.75 on dirt, more on the bank) limits cornering, braking and drive, and the tyres scrub when the lateral demand exceeds grip. Minimap bottom-left; drag to orbit the chase camera.
Course. Flat start → 11% climb (rider stands, root-log and a rock step) → crest → flat left-hander (grip-limited, the rider brakes to it) → rollers with a log → braking-bump washboard → 35° left berm (the bank carries the load, suspension takes the g) → 18% rock garden with boulders, a rock shelf step-down and a log → a 40 cm log-over → two G-outs → 0.75 m kicker onto a 5.5 m tabletop → 1 m huck to flat. Speed carried into the lip decides how far the tabletop is cleared; a second flat corner follows the landing.
